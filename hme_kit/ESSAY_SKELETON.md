# Does cheaper trade make big regions bigger? A hump-shaped home market effect

1. (Thesis, 3 sentences) The lecture model predicts that falling trade costs concentrate firms in the larger market. That is true of where firms end up, but not of how hard they are pulled: the pull peaks at intermediate φ and fades towards free trade, so with any location cost the HME reverses. Technological change (digital goods, AI) is pushing φ upward, so policy built on "big regions keep winning" is at risk.
2. The model in one paragraph (λ* formula, corner at θ ≥ 1/(1+φ)).
3. The twist: pull ∝ (1−φ) near free trade; Figure (a) and (b); what κ stands for.
4. Evidence from the literature: Davis (1998), Head-Mayer-Ries (2002), Hanson-Xiang (2004): HME is conditional and fragile; consistent with a conditional hump.
5. Policy: place-based policy under falling trade costs; what the model cannot say (no labour mobility, no endogenous κ).
6. Limits and next step: calibrate κ with real data (ONS or Eurostat regional data).
